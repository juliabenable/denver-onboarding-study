Where the brainstorm stopped

Almost everything we brought was stock: things that say who you are. The one idea that was flow, Ask Denver, failed on frequency. Tony's idea of taking in all the Denver content creators already post was the first real flow idea, and the conversation ended there.

What we have: who you are

  • Denver onboarding: arrive to a place already full of your things.
  • Top 5: curation as your social currency.
  • Lists, profile, taste match: identity and polish.

What's missing: why today

  • Something new every day that nobody had to post for.
  • Something to do with people, beyond reading their picks.
  • A reason that comes to you, so opening the app isn't a chore.

Six shifts in how to think about it

Each idea below comes from one or more of these moves. They're the useful part even if every idea gets cut.

From recs
To moments

The unit is a post, at a place, at a time. A rec is just a moment someone kept.

From pull
To push

Benable comes to you when something matters. The app is where you go deeper.

From places
To plans

Social life happens when people go somewhere together, more than when they like a spot.

From followers
To foresight

Status comes from taste that proved right early, so a regular person can beat a big account.

From strangers
To tables

Shared taste is a reason to meet. The mission is connecting people, and places are where they meet.

From an app
To a layer

Benable's trust graph can power the AI everyone already asks, instead of competing with it.

From Monday, pushed further

The Pulse

Benable reads every post Denver creators share on Instagram and TikTok, works out the place, and ranks where people actually went this week. The count is agreement between people, not stars.

Further than Monday
Every place gets a living timeline in video: last summer, the new chef, the Friday line. And a Rising shelf catches places a week before they're everywhere.
Why you come back
It's different every day. You check it like the weather.
Why it's new
Counting agreement across creators, platforms and days means reading video at scale. Two years ago that was a research project.
Borrows from
Tony's ingest idea, Corner's popular top 10, Instagram's map done right (fresh, by place, not only people you follow).
Hard question
What is a "post" when stories disappear and can't be embedded? Probably the last 48 hours of reels and TikToks.

Out now

Turn on "out" for three hours. Your people see where you are, and it switches itself off. You only see others while you're sharing too.

Past the brainstorm

New ideas. None of these came up on Monday.

New

Your Denver writes itself

With your OK, Benable reads your camera roll, your stories and your receipts, and quietly builds your Denver: every place you've been, when, and how often. You choose what goes public.

The big move
Your camera roll is the biggest record of lived experience nobody has claimed. Onboarding drops to ten seconds, and it never goes stale.
Why you come back
Every week: "3 new places this week, keep them?" And always: "your year so far".
Why it's new
On-phone AI can now read thousands of photos and match places in seconds.
Borrows from
Apple and Google Photos memories, the Letterboxd diary, Swarm's check-in history, Olivia's mini guides (AI working for you).
Hard question
It has to feel like your diary, never like being tracked.

Plans, not places

The social unit becomes going. Benable notices that you, Maya and Sam all saved the same spot and all have Thursday free, and it suggests the night. Afterwards the plan turns into a shared memory with everyone's photos.

The big move
Saves finally do something. Tony's point that saving is infinite becomes a supply of nights out.
Why you come back

Why it's new
Taste twins, acted on. No app joins "we both want to go" with "we're both free".
Borrows from
Our taste twins idea, Partiful, shared albums.
Hard question
Calendars are private. A "who's free Thursday" tap may be enough.

New

Called it

Every save is timestamped. When a place takes off in the Pulse, the people who saved it first get the credit. Status in Denver comes from taste that proved right early, not from followers.

The big move
A status game that a regular person or a small creator can win. Limited picks, like Top 5, keep it honest.
Why you come back
To see if your calls are paying off, and to make the next one before anyone else.
Why it's new
No platform measures taste foresight. Benable has save dates, and the Pulse shows what the city posted later.
Borrows from
Strava's local legends, Foursquare mayors, "I liked them before they were famous".
Hard question
People saving everything to farm credit. Scarce picks fix most of it.

Regulars

Denver places can see their true regulars, proven by posts, visits and saves, and thank them: a first taste of the new menu, a seat at the soft opening.

The big move
Local fans meet Benable's brand business. Places pay for fans with proof, which is cheaper and warmer than ads.
Why you come back
Perks, and a reason to keep your Denver up to date.
Why it's new
Proof that you're a real fan, pulled from what you already post and photograph.
Borrows from
Your "receipts that I really love this" idea, Tony's MD Solar Sciences answerers, Yelp Elite events.
Hard question
Places have to want to do this every month, not once.

City rituals

Denver shares moments: first snow, patio season, Red Rocks opening night, Broncos Sundays, beer festival week. For 48 hours each one becomes a city board built from everyone's posts. Then it closes and goes into Denver's year.

The big move
Flow with a deadline. These are moments you miss if you don't check, and at year end they add up to a Denver Wrapped.
Why you come back
Two or three times a month, the city is doing something together.
Why it's new
AI can assemble a whole city's posts into one story while the moment is still happening.
Borrows from
BeReal's daily moment, Snap's Our Story, Spotify Wrapped.
Hard question
Rituals are seasonal. It needs a planned calendar of moments.

Tables

Every Wednesday, Benable seats six strangers whose saves overlap, at a place one of them loves. An ambassador hosts. You show up and meet people who already share your taste.

The big move
It's the mission taken literally: connecting people over lived experiences. Shared taste becomes a reason to meet, not just a stat.
Why you come back
Your Wednesday table, the people you met there, and the plans that follow.
Why it's new
Matching on the real places you love instead of a personality quiz.
Borrows from
Timeleft's dinners with strangers, supper clubs, Benable's events strategy.
Hard question
Safety and running it every week. It turns Benable into an events company in part.

Your taste agent

A personal AI that knows your Denver and whose taste you trust. It texts you two things a week, makes the plan and holds the table. You rarely open the app. The app is where you tune it.

The big move
The daily reason is that it comes to you. It's Tony's "very low effort, high benefit" taken as far as it goes.
Why you come back
You don't have to. Opening the app becomes a choice, not the price of entry.
Why it's new
An agent that acts for you, grounded in people you trust instead of the open web.
Borrows from
The Instinct and Muse assistants Tony mentioned, Olivia's mini guides, concierge services.
Hard question
It has to be right most of the time, or it's spam.

The human layer for AI

When anyone asks any AI where to take their mom in Denver, the answer quotes Benable: "6 Denver locals recommend this, and here's what Maya said." Benable stops competing with AI and becomes what AI cites.

The big move
It answers "why wouldn't I just ask AI?" by turning it around: the AI asks Benable. Creators get credit everywhere people ask.
Why you come back
Your picks and vouches reach far more people than your followers.
Why it's new
AI answers need fresh, trusted, human signal. That's exactly what the Pulse, Called it and vouches produce.
Borrows from
Our LLM answer visibility work, Reddit licensing its posts to AI companies.
Hard question
It's a distribution bet more than an experience, and it needs volume first.

The Pulse, Called it and Plans

The Pulse gives the daily reason, and nobody has to post anything new for it. Called it gives locals and small creators a reason to save early, which makes the Pulse smarter. Plans turn saves into nights out, which is the lived experience Tony asked for.

Each one feeds the next, so the loop runs on what people already do: post, save, go out.

  1. Creators post about DenverOn Instagram and TikTok, like they already do
  2. The Pulse ranks where people wentFlow: different every day
  3. You save what's risingSaving is cheap and infinite
  4. You get credit when it takes offCalled it: status from foresight
  5. Saves turn into plans with friendsPlans: taste twins, acted on
  6. You go, and you post about itA lived experience
